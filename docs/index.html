What it is

Two engines. One body.

Most apps treat vibration as a notification buzz. Haptiq splits it into the two things a body actually feels at a concert: the hit, and the hum underneath it.

ENGINE 01 · KICK

The kick hits.

A sudden jump in low-end energy that falls right back down. Haptiq fires one sharp thump on the jump, then stays quiet until the energy actually falls before it can fire again — which is why risers and sound-effect swells never fake it out.

  • AdaptiveTracks how loud each song's own bass gets and moves its trigger points to match, from a quiet ballad to a heavy drop.
  • Character, SNAP → HEAVYEvery kick is a decaying strike-train: a 35ms snap tick or a 240ms heavy thump that keeps the motor moving through surface damping.
  • 808-awareIf the bass swells right back after a kick, the rumble returns early and softer — like the real tail of one sound doing both jobs.
ENGINE 02 · BASS

The bass hums.

The bassline becomes one continuous rumble that rises and falls with the music, not an on-off buzz. Louder bass pushes harder; when it fades, the motor fades with it, like a speaker cone following the wave.

  • SidechainWhen a kick fires, the rumble ducks out of the way for a fraction of a second so the thump stays distinct.
  • Any motorHaptiq checks once what your motor can do — composed pulses, timed amplitude, or plain on-off — and speaks in its own language.
  • Full manual controlPrefer to set it yourself? Switch adaptive off and every threshold is yours.
Under the hood

Real parts, no magic.

No black-box AI, no cloud processing — just audio analysis running on your phone, tuned by hand against real songs.

20/s

Analysis rate

The audio spectrum is read about twenty times a second — enough to catch every beat without draining the battery.

Δ

Behavior, not bands

Kicks are found by how energy moves — a sharp rise, a real fall — not just by which frequencies happen to be loud.

3

Native rendering

Thud and click primitives on modern motors, amplitude waveforms on older ones, duty-cycling on basic ones.

Kt

Kotlin + Compose

Jetpack Compose UI, Media3/ExoPlayer playback, Room for your library. Standard Android, no exotic dependencies.

1×

Calibrated once

A guided wizard measures your motor once and stores a strength profile for your exact device.

0

Zero network

No account, no ads, no analytics. The audio-capture permission is used only to read Haptiq's own playback.

Straight answers

What Haptiq is not.

Not a subwoofer

A subwoofer moves air; a phone motor moves millimeters. Haptiq uses sharp timing and real silence between hits to make your hands believe what your chest would feel.

Not the same on every phone

Motors range from precise linear actuators to basic spinning weights. Haptiq detects what yours can do and adapts — a better motor only makes it feel better.

Not watching you

No account, no ads, no analytics, no network use. Your music, your phone, full stop.

Not finished

Haptiq is in active development, versioned one feel-test at a time. The commit list below is live from the repository.

The journey

From one buzz to an engine.

Haptiq started in July 2026 with a simple question: phones have had vibration motors forever — why does music never use them well?

Swipe or use the arrows — six releases, one screen.

The editor became a real tool

Pan, pinch-zoom, drag-to-scrub audition, an "at playhead" button, shareable kick maps, and a mini player that opens with a tap.

Character and teaching

The SNAP/PUNCH/THUMP/HEAVY kick-character axis, a beat-grid drift fix, and the first waveform editor.
